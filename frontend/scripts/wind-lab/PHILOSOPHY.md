# Ground Truth

*An algorithmic philosophy for wind drawn on any ground.*

A streak of wind has no colour of its own. What the eye reads is a relation: this mark is the colour beneath it, carrying more light. Dark mode found the relation by circumstance. Its night map is so close to black that any light laid on it reads as a brighter shade of the hue below. Ground Truth keeps that relation as its single invariant and lets every absolute value be solved again for each ground: lightness, chroma, opacity, the depth of the ground itself. One wind, one simulation, one body of law, three grounds, and three different sets of numbers that all say the same sentence. Colour is the most relative medium, so the work holds the relation still and lets the numbers move.

The simulation is theme-blind on purpose. A streamfunction is built from a steady synoptic flow, a seeded low and slow meanders, and the wind is read from its curl. The field therefore has no sources or sinks, and particles neither crowd nor thin out by accident. Tens of thousands of marks are carried at the engine's own pace and die by its law: fast marks are short-lived, slow marks linger, and no mark lives fewer than six frames. Each is stamped as an elongated sprite with a casing into an 8-bit trail that fades by flooring, so no ghost outlives its frame. Speed is carried twice, in hue and in the length and pace of the trail, so the information never rests on colour alone. Every constant is traced back to the engine it imitates. The algorithm should read as a meticulously crafted instrument, the product of deep computational expertise, where nothing is decorative and nothing is approximate without saying so.

The law has five clauses, each paid for by a failure. **Light only adds**: a streak moves toward its own colour at full light by the square of its brightness. Dark's own formula behaves this way only because its ground is black; on any lighter ground its fading tails darken the paper, so the clause is written in the form that holds on every ground. **Room**: the ground must sit low enough for a step of dark's size to fit beneath the lightest colour that still holds its hue. The ground is dimmed by one factor in linear light, as dusk dims a landscape, so the bands keep their relations. Equalising them erased the lightness that colour-blind eyes depend on. **Names**: a dimmed orange becomes brown and a dimmed yellow olive, colours that exist only beneath a brighter surround, so those grounds are held light while blues may sink to navy and reds to maroon without changing their names. **Relation per ground**: land and sea each receive their own paint, because a relation is to the ground actually beneath the mark. **Colour floor**: no streak buys lightness with its hue. Each clause is a parameter only in the sense that a master calibrates it. The defaults are the result of painstaking sweeps, not guesses.

Beauty here is measured, and the algorithm carries its own instruments. Every few seconds it reads its frame the way a bench reads a crop. It reports the median lightness step of a streak above its ground, the share of streak pixels that are lighter, where the picture ends against the wash line, the APCA contrast of a streak's core, and how far the streaks shift the average colour of the sea. Beside them sit the colour-blind floor between neighbouring Beaufort bands and the weakest chroma on the streak path. A seed fixes every frame, and a design is judged across seeds, never from one flattering still. This is a master-level implementation in the old sense: refined through countless iterations, each failure written down and turned into a clause.

The work lives in its process. Each run is a weather system that has never existed: a different coast, a different low, a different afternoon. The grounds receive it under the same law and answer in their own colours: the night map in neon, the sand in sea glass and citrus, the paper in a dusk that leaves room for light. A viewer who moves a dial is not choosing a style. They are asking the law a question, such as how deep the dusk must be, how far a streak may step, or which colours may sink without being renamed, and the frame answers at once. Whoever implements it should treat every choice as the next master in a line of painstaking calibration: the motion exact, the colour solved, the measurement honest.
